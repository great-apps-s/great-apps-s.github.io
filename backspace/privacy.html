Privacy Policy

Backspace — published by Sergiu Silosiev

Effective 16 October 2026

Backspace helps you clear space on your iPhone: it finds duplicates, near-identical shots and large videos, shrinks videos, and shows you photos taken on this day in earlier years so you can keep or remove them. To do that it has to look at your photo library — the most personal thing on the phone. This page says exactly what happens.

Short version: your photos and videos never leave your iPhone. Every scan, comparison and compression runs on the device. We have no server that receives them, no account, no analytics and no advertising. The only outside service the app talks to handles the subscription, and it never sees a single photo.

There is no account

Backspace has no sign-up and no login. We never ask for your name, email address, phone number or date of birth, and we have no way to link what the app does to who you are.

Your photo library

Why the app asks for access. Finding duplicates and large files means reading your library. When you grant access, iOS lets the app read your photos and videos and lets it delete or add items — but only with your confirmation each time.

What the app does with it, on the device:

  • reads the date, size and type of each item to sort it into categories (similar shots, screenshots, large videos and so on);
  • builds small previews and compares them, using Apple's on-device Vision framework, to tell which shots look alike and which of them is the sharpest;
  • for videos you choose to shrink, writes a smaller copy back to your library with the original date and place, and then removes the original;
  • for "On this day", picks photos and videos taken on today's date in earlier years.

What it does not do:

  • it does not upload any photo, video, preview or part of one anywhere — not to us and not to anyone else;
  • it does not use your photos to train any model;
  • it does not sell, share or show them to anyone;
  • it does not read the content of your messages, notes or other apps.

If your library is in iCloud Photos, iOS may download an original from Apple's iCloud when the app needs the full file (for example, to shrink a video). That download is between your iPhone and Apple, under Apple's privacy policy; it does not pass through us.

Limited access. If you give the app access to selected photos only, it works with that selection and nothing else. You can change this at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Photos → Backspace.

Face data and biometrics

To suggest which shot in a series to keep, the app checks — on the device — whether a picture contains a face at all, because a shot with a face is usually the one people want. That check produces a yes or no and nothing more.

Backspace does not perform face recognition and does not collect biometric data. In particular:

  • we do not create, extract or store a faceprint, a face template, a face embedding, or a scan of face geometry;
  • we do not identify anyone, verify anyone's identity, group photos by person, or match a face against any database;
  • the comparison of similar shots works on the picture as a whole, not on faces, and exists only in the app's memory — it is discarded when the app closes, and it is never written to storage or sent anywhere;
  • we do not sell, share or otherwise disclose anything about faces to anyone.

We therefore do not collect "biometric identifiers" or "biometric information" as those terms are used in the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act, the Texas Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act, or the Washington biometric privacy law, nor "sensitive personal information" of that kind under California law.

When you delete something

Deleting is always your decision: nothing is removed until you confirm it, and iOS asks you to confirm again in its own dialog.

  • Items you delete go to the Recently Deleted album of the Photos app, where iOS keeps them for up to 30 days before removing them for good. Until then you can restore them from Photos → Albums → Recently Deleted.
  • The storage is freed only once they leave Recently Deleted — you can empty it sooner yourself.
  • Favourites and items in shared albums are left out of selections by default; you can change that in the app's Settings.
  • If iCloud Photos is on, iOS removes a deleted item from your other devices too. That is how iCloud Photos works, not something the app adds.

What the app remembers about itself

On your device, in ordinary app settings: whether you finished onboarding, your protection settings, how much space the app has freed so far, which photos the daily pick has already shown you, today's "On this day" choices (they reset the next day), whether the reminder and paywall screens were shown, the random installation ID described below, and your subscription status. Photos are referred to only by the identifiers iOS gives them, and none of it leaves the device except as described under Subscriptions and payments.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, they are scheduled on your iPhone itself. There is no push server, and no notification token leaves the device. You can turn them off in Settings → Notifications → Backspace.

Subscriptions and payments

Purchases are made through Apple. We never see your card number, and Apple does not give it to us.

Subscription status is managed for us by Adapty (https://adapty.io/privacy). So that a purchase is recognised after a reinstall or on another device, Adapty receives:

  • the App Store purchase receipt and purchase history;
  • a random identifier created by the app at first launch — it identifies this installation, not you;
  • the identifier iOS gives to apps from one publisher (IDFV), the device model, iOS version, language, region, time zone and app version.

We have configured Adapty not to collect the advertising identifier (IDFA) and not to record your IP address in the subscription profile. Adapty receives no photos, no previews and nothing about the contents of your library.

Apple's own handling of your purchase is covered by Apple's privacy policy.

Analytics, advertising and tracking

Backspace contains no analytics SDK, no advertising SDK, no attribution SDK and no crash-reporting SDK. We do not track you across other companies' apps or websites and we do not use the advertising identifier, so the app never shows Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt — there is nothing to ask permission for.

Deleting your data

Because there is no account and no copy of your photos anywhere, there is nothing to request: removing the app erases everything it kept on the device.

If you want the purchase records held by our subscription provider removed, write to sergiusil@proton.me and say roughly when you bought; we will pass the request on. Apple's own records of your purchase are governed by Apple.

Children

Backspace is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly process the personal data of a child. The app collects no personal data from anyone beyond what is described under Subscriptions and payments. If you believe a child's data has reached us, write to sergiusil@proton.me and we will act on it.

Write to sergiusil@proton.me. Without an account we usually hold nothing that identifies you, so the honest answer to "what do you have on me" is normally "nothing".

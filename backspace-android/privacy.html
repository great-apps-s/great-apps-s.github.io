Privacy Policy

Backspace for Android — published by GREAT APPS

Effective 31 October 2026

Backspace helps you clear space on your Android phone: it finds duplicates, similar shots, blurry photos, screenshots, large videos and media saved by messengers, shrinks videos, tidies your contacts, keeps private photos in a locked vault and checks your phone's hardware. To do that it has to look at your photo library and, if you allow it, your contacts — the most personal things on the phone. This page says exactly what happens.

Short version: your photos, videos and contacts never leave your phone. Every scan, comparison and compression runs on the device. We have no server that receives them, no account, no analytics and no advertising. The app talks to two outside services: our subscription provider, which never sees a photo, and Cloudflare, only when you run the internet speed test.

There is no account

Backspace has no sign-up and no login. We never ask for your name, email address, phone number or date of birth, and we have no way to link what the app does to who you are.

Your photos and videos

Why the app asks for access. Finding duplicates and large files means reading your library. When you allow access to all photos and videos, Android lets the app read them. Removing an item or replacing it with a smaller copy always goes through Android's own confirmation dialog — the app cannot delete anything without it.

What the app does with it, on the device:

  • reads the date, size, type and folder of each item to sort it into categories (duplicates, similar shots, screenshots, large videos, media saved by messengers such as WhatsApp or Telegram);
  • builds small previews in memory to tell which photos are blurry;
  • for videos you choose to shrink and photos you choose to convert to HEIC, writes a smaller copy back to your gallery with the original date, place and favorite mark, and then moves the original to the trash. To keep the place where a photo was taken, the app asks Android for the location stored in the photo itself — it never asks for your current location;
  • for "On this day", picks photos and videos taken on today's date in earlier years.

What it does not do:

  • it does not upload any photo, video, preview or part of one anywhere — not to us and not to anyone else;
  • it does not use your photos to train any model;
  • it does not sell, share or show them to anyone;
  • it does not read your messages or chats. Media saved by messengers is found in the shared folders those apps keep in your gallery, the same files any gallery app shows.

Limited access. If you allow access to selected photos only, the app works with that selection and nothing else. You can change this at any time in Android Settings → Apps → Backspace → Permissions.

No face recognition, no biometric data

Backspace does not look for faces and does not perform face recognition. Similar shots are grouped by when they were taken and by file size, not by what is in them. We do not create, extract or store a faceprint, a face template, a face embedding or a scan of face geometry, and we do not identify anyone.

When you open the private vault, unlocking is done by Android itself — your fingerprint, face or screen lock. The app only receives Android's answer that you were recognised; your fingerprint or face never reaches the app. The phone check only asks Android which kinds of unlock your phone supports and whether one is set up.

We therefore do not collect "biometric identifiers" or "biometric information" as those terms are used in the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act, the Texas Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act, or the Washington biometric privacy law, nor "sensitive personal information" of that kind under California law.

When you delete something

Deleting is always your decision: nothing is removed until you confirm it, and Android asks you to confirm again in its own dialog.

  • Items you delete go to the trash of your gallery, where Android keeps them for 30 days before removing them for good. Until then you can restore them from the trash.
  • The storage is freed only once they leave the trash — you can empty it sooner, from the app or from your gallery.
  • Favorites are left out of selections by default; you can change that in the app's Settings.

Your contacts

If you open Tidy contacts and allow access, the app reads the contacts on your phone to find cards for the same person and cards with no name or no way to reach anyone.

  • Merging keeps one card and moves the other cards' phone numbers, emails, addresses and other details — including notes — into it, then deletes the others. Deleting a card removes it from your contacts and from every device that syncs that account. Both are done only after you confirm.
  • Backups are .vcf files kept in the app's own storage on your phone. They leave the phone only if you share one yourself, to wherever you choose.
  • Contacts you move to the private vault are kept there encrypted and removed from your contacts.

Nothing about your contacts is sent to us or to anyone else.

The private vault

Photos, videos and contacts you move to the vault are encrypted and stored in the app's own storage on your phone. The key that opens them is kept in Android's secure hardware key store and cannot be copied off the phone. The vault is not included in your phone's backups and does not move to a new phone, and deleting the app deletes the vault. When you move a photo into the vault, the original goes to your gallery's trash for 30 days; if a cloud photo service (such as Google Photos) already backed it up, that copy stays there until you remove it. The vault screen cannot be captured in screenshots or shown in the recent-apps preview.

Apps on your phone

If you open Apps and turn on usage access in Android Settings, the app reads, on the device, how much space each installed app takes and when it was last opened, so it can show which ones you no longer use. Uninstalling always goes through Android's own dialog. This information is not stored beyond what is on the screen and never leaves the phone.

Phone check

The phone check plays test tones, shows test colors, and reads the motion, compass, proximity and other sensors while it is open. The microphone test only measures how loud the sound is at that moment — nothing is recorded or saved. The PDF report is created on your phone and leaves it only if you share it.

Internet speed test

When you start the speed test, the app downloads and uploads test data to Cloudflare's speed test servers (speed.cloudflare.com) to measure your connection. Like any website you visit, Cloudflare sees your IP address while the test runs; it is handled under Cloudflare's privacy policy (https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/). The test sends nothing else about you, and the app does not keep the results.

What the app remembers about itself

On your device, in the app's own storage: whether you finished onboarding, your protection settings, how much space the app has freed so far, what is in your basket, which photos the daily pick has already shown you, today's "On this day" choices, whether the reminder and paywall screens were shown, the random installation ID described below, and your subscription status. Photos are referred to only by the identifiers Android gives them. This app state is not included in your phone's cloud backup, and none of it leaves the device except as described under Subscriptions and payments.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, the daily pick reminder is scheduled on your phone itself, and progress of long tasks such as shrinking videos is shown while they run. There is no push server, and no notification token leaves the device. You can turn them off in Android Settings → Apps → Backspace → Notifications.

The home-screen widget

The free-space widget reads your phone's storage figures on the device and shows them on your home screen. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Subscriptions and payments

Purchases are made through Google Play. We never see your card number, and Google does not give it to us.

Subscription status is managed for us by Adapty (https://adapty.io/privacy). So that a purchase is recognised after a reinstall or on another device, Adapty receives:

  • your Google Play purchase history for this app;
  • a random identifier created by the app at first launch — it identifies this installation, not you;
  • basic device details: the device model, Android version, language, region, time zone and app version;
  • the Google Play install referrer — a short note of which store page or link led to the install.

We have configured Adapty not to collect the advertising ID and not to record your IP address in the subscription profile. Adapty receives no photos, no previews, no contacts and nothing about the contents of your library.

Google's own handling of your purchase is covered by Google's privacy policy.

Analytics, advertising and tracking

Backspace contains no analytics SDK, no advertising SDK and no crash-reporting SDK. We do not track you across other companies' apps or websites and we do not use the advertising ID.

Deleting your data

Because there is no account and no copy of your photos or contacts anywhere, there is nothing to request: removing the app erases everything it kept on the phone, including the vault and contact backups. Take anything you want to keep out of the vault before removing the app.

If you want the purchase records held by our subscription provider removed, write to gr.apps@zohomail.eu and say roughly when you bought; we will pass the request on. Google's own records of your purchase are governed by Google.

Children

Backspace is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly process the personal data of a child. The app collects no personal data from anyone beyond what is described under Subscriptions and payments. If you believe a child's data has reached us, write to gr.apps@zohomail.eu and we will act on it.
